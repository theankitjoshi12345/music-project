# P#7 Task 2 - Behavioral Segments

Copy each section below into the matching **Part 1: Behavioral Segments** fields in the P#7 report template. The segments are behavioral: they describe how musicians prepare, retain corrections, and use scores rather than demographic groups.

## Segment 1 - Preparation-First Internalizers

**Description:** These musicians reduce dependence on the score by preparing difficult material before rehearsal, retaining corrections mentally, and practicing until responses become automatic.

**Key Behaviors:**

- Prepare difficult passages before rehearsal and practice them repeatedly.
- Use mental recall and muscle memory to apply corrections.

**Pain Points:**

- Unfamiliar or complex material must sometimes be performed before there is enough time to internalize it.
- Several rapid corrections can exceed what they can retain and practice between run-throughs.

**Evidence:**

- “No struggle, I memorize the music prior.” - Interview 2
- “Yeah. I remember it, and then I practice it the right way until it becomes muscle memory.” - Interview 1

## Segment 2 - Reactive Score Annotators

**Description:** These musicians use the score as an external memory by adding measure numbers, transition cues, rhythm reminders, accidentals, and other context-specific marks during or after rehearsal.

**Key Behaviors:**

- Mark important measures, transitions, rests, and performance problems on the score.
- Use a quick temporary mark during rehearsal, then return later to add or interpret a useful cue.

**Pain Points:**

- Rehearsal can move too quickly to write a complete note without jeopardizing the next entrance.
- Too many notes, or a mark without context, can clutter the score and weaken later recall.

**Evidence:**

- “I write the measure numbers on important spots and transitions. I also write ear-mapping signals over long multi-measure rests.” - Interview 7
- “Sometimes, on horn at least I will mark it if something’s flat so I don’t have to go through the mental problem.” - Interview 6

## Segment 3 - Format-Constrained Score Navigators

**Description:** These musicians spend rehearsal effort accessing, moving through, or visually reading the score. They need reliable access, legible notation, and navigation that fits the performance context.

**Key Behaviors:**

- Download score files for reliable access and search for the assigned part when needed.
- Zoom, scroll, turn pages, or adjust the display while playing.

**Pain Points:**

- A score may not open when connectivity is unreliable, and shared storage can slow access to the correct part.
- Cramped or obstructed displays and page movement can interrupt performance.

**Evidence:**

- “If you don't download it and you try to open it at the football games, you're not getting it. It's not happening.” - Interview 1
- “MuseScore doesn't have a good zoom functionality to it and the UI gets in the way of the notes. No markup either for finger positions.” - Interview 4
